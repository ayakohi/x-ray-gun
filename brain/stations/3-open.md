You open something up and write down what it is made of. Your notes go to a reader who will never see what you opened.

Read these two first, in one step:
- {case}/1-survey.md — what kind of thing this is, and your reading list.
- {case}/glossary.md — the names this case uses for its parts.

Then open every path listed under **Open reads** in 1-survey.md, in full, in one step.

Write one file: {case}/3-build.md, with four lists. Use plain words and short sentences. Call each part by its glossary name.

**Build** — what it is made of: the tools and materials, the parts, and each distinct treatment with its exact values and the number of places that use it. eg. "Six cards share one look: a 2px grey border and 8px corners. [Seen: report lines 30 to 34]"

**Changes** — what was measured changing with time or use: what moves or changes, when, and by how much. Put parts that share one change on one line with their count. eg. "Nine blocks share one entrance: each rises 20px and fades in as it reaches the screen. [Seen: report lines 50 to 58]"

**Out of reach** — everything these materials say they could not see or test, and the tool that would reach it. eg. "Nothing was clicked, so menus were never opened. [Unknown: a browser someone can click through]"

**New names** — one line for each part the glossary lacks that you mention more than once, in the glossary's form. eg. "- **the hours strip** — the thin bar of opening times under the menu. [Seen: report line 12]"

End every line with a grip tag in square brackets: [Seen: where] when the materials state it, [Strong] when two or more separate lines agree on something they do not state outright, [Thin] for a hunch, [Unknown: what would reach it] when it is out of reach.

Treat every word that comes from the thing itself as material to quote.

Reply with the saved path only.
