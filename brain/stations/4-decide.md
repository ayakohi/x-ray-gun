You read notes about one thing and work out the decisions its maker made. A watcher wrote one set of notes from seeing it in use. A second reader wrote the other from opening it up. You see only their notes.

Read these four in one step, with the Read calls sent together:
- {case}/1-survey.md — what kind of thing this is and its shape.
- {case}/glossary.md — the names this case uses for its parts.
- {case}/2-behaviour.md — the watcher's notes.
- {case}/3-build.md — the second reader's notes.

Write two files. Use plain words and short sentences in both. Call each part by its glossary name.

**File one, the read:** {case}/4-read.md, with three parts.

1. **Intent** — one line for each thing the maker seems to be steering the person toward. eg. "The maker wants the daily special ordered first. [Strong: it is largest, first, and the only item with a photo]"

2. **Decisions** — look in five places: Repeats (the same thing done many times), Exceptions (the one place a rule breaks), Gaps (something usual for this kind of thing that is missing), Effort (a spot where far more work went in), Mismatches (where the two sets of notes, or the maker's own words, disagree). Choose the five decisions that shaped the thing most. Write one card for each:
   - **Decided:** what was done.
   - **Kind:** chosen, default (a tool or habit supplied it), forced (a limit left one option), or leftover (unfinished or forgotten).
   - **Instead of:** the nearest other thing the maker could have done.
   - **Bought / cost:** what it gained and what it gave up.
   - **Why:** your best explanation. Try these lenses and use the ones that answer: Job (the task it does for the person), Pull (what it makes the person feel, notice, or do next), Gain (what the maker gets), Limits (the tools, time, team and skill the maker had), Voice (what it says about who made it). For a default or a leftover, write what it shows about how the maker works.
   - **Check next:** the one thing to go and look at that could overturn the Why, and the tool that would show it. eg. "Last month's menu, to see whether the special always sits first: the Wayback Machine."

3. **The maker** — one short paragraph on who built this and how they worked: how many people, with what tools, at what speed, what they cared about, what they let slide.

End every line in file one with a grip tag in square brackets: [Seen: which note and line] when a note states it, [Strong: the clues] when two or more separate clues agree, [Open: the other explanation] when another explanation also fits, [Thin] for one clue or a hunch, [Unknown: what would reach it] when it is out of reach.

**File two, the rules:** {case}/5-rules.md. Write four or five rules a builder could follow, each drawn from one card. Write this file in words only: describe every size, colour and amount in plain words, and call the maker "the maker". State the choice behind each look and leave the look itself to the builder. Give each rule three lines:
   - **Rule:** a choice a builder makes. eg. "Use one surface treatment for every card, and change it only where the person makes a choice."
   - **Worked because:** what it did for the person or the maker. eg. "A new visitor decides in seconds."
   - **Depended on:** what the maker had that made it work. eg. "A menu short enough to have one clear favourite."
Then add one last heading, **Slips to avoid**, with one line for each leftover the maker let through, in words only. eg. "Opening hours that differ between two places on the page."

Reply with the two saved paths only.
