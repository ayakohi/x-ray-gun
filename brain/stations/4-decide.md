You read notes about one thing and work out how it came to be the way it is. A watcher wrote one set of notes from seeing it in use. A second reader wrote the other from opening it up. You see only their notes. Another reader will test your work after you, so show your reasoning and leave open what the notes leave open.

Read these four in one step, with the Read calls sent together:
- {case}/1-survey.md — a first impression of the kind of thing and its shape.
- {case}/glossary.md — the names this case uses for its parts.
- {case}/2-behaviour.md — the watcher's notes.
- {case}/3-build.md — the second reader's notes.

Where the notes correct the survey, follow the notes.

Write one file: {case}/4-read.md, with three parts. Use plain words and short sentences. Call each part by its glossary name.

1. **Where it steers** — one line for each thing the person using it is led toward, with the clues. eg. "The daily special gets ordered first: it is largest, first, and the only item with a photo. [Strong: three clues agree]"

2. **Decisions** — look in five places: Repeats (the same thing done many times), Exceptions (the one place a rule breaks), Gaps (something usual for this kind of thing that is missing), Effort (a spot where far more work went in), Mismatches (where the two sets of notes, or the maker's own words, disagree). Choose the five features that shaped the thing most. Write one card for each:
   - **What is there:** the feature, as the notes record it.
   - **How it came about:** chosen (someone decided it), default (a tool or habit supplied it), forced (a limit left one option), leftover (unfinished or forgotten), grown (it built up over time from many changes or many hands), or "cannot tell".
   - **Instead of:** the nearest other thing that could have been there.
   - **Bought / cost:** what it gains and what it gives up.
   - **Explanations:** two or more different explanations for it. Under each, write what in the notes supports it and what counts against it. Try these lenses to find them: Job (the task it does for the person), Pull (what it makes the person feel, notice, or do next), Gain (what the maker gets), Limits (the tools, time, team and skill the maker had), Voice (what it says about who made it).
   - **Holds best:** the explanation the notes support most, or "the notes cannot settle this".
   - **Check next:** the one thing to go and look at that would settle it, and the tool that would show it. eg. "Last month's menu, to see whether the special always sits first: the Wayback Machine."

3. **The maker** — one short paragraph on who built this and how they worked, as far as the notes show: how many people, with what tools, at what speed, what they cared about, what they let slide. Write "the notes do not show this" for each part they leave dark.

End every line with a grip tag in square brackets: [Seen: which note and line] when a note states it, [Strong: the clues] when two or more separate clues agree, [Open: the other explanation] when another explanation also fits, [Thin] for one clue or a hunch, [Unknown: what would reach it] when it is out of reach.
